**Fermentation** is the biochemical transformation of organic compounds driven by enzymes (complex proteins, RNA ribozymes, or their complexes) or microorganisms. In tea processing, the reaction occurs between compounds naturally present within the plant tissue itself.

## 1. Raw Material Selection Criteria

* **Tannin concentration:** young, tender leaves contain the highest levels of tannins. As leaves mature and coarsen, tannin concentration drops sharply.
* **Botanical base (edible-fruit plants):** apple, strawberry, sour cherry, chokeberry (aronia), raspberry, blackcurrant, pear, plum, blackthorn (sloe), cornelian cherry, oleaster, grape, quince.

| Harvest Season | Leaf Characteristics | Fermentation Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Spring** | Tender tissue, soft cellular structure | High (easiest to process) |
| **Summer** | Peak accumulation of organic compounds | Medium (optimal aromatic balance) |
| **Autumn** | Coarse, dense leaves (especially orchard trees) | Low (requires extended processing) |

## 2. Stage 1. Harvesting & Sorting

* **Harvest conditions:** dry weather; prioritize shade-grown plants as their leaves retain higher moisture.
* **Sorting:** discard over-dried, diseased, or mechanically damaged leaves.

<div class="warning-box">
<strong>Constraint:</strong> Do not wash the leaves prior to processing, as water removes natural surface microflora required for fermentation. Wash only heavily soiled leaves.
</div>

## 3. Stage 2. Withering

The objective is to reduce excess moisture and soften the central leaf vein.

| Method | Procedure | Environment & Duration | Output Profile |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option A (On Cloth)** | Spread indoors on cotton or linen cloth in a `3–5 cm` layer; turn periodically. Keep away from direct sunlight and drafts. | Temp: `20–24 °C`<br>Humidity: `~70%`<br>Time: `~12 hours` | Classic, balanced flavor profile. |
| **Option B (In Bag)** | Place leaves in a tied cotton or linen bag; twist, knead, compress, and shake periodically. | Sealed microclimate inside fabric | Ideal for tough leaves or overly dry/humid rooms. Produces a bold, pu-erh-like brew. |

<div class="warning-box">
<strong>Time Constraint:</strong> Do not leave withered leaves piled up for more than 1–2 days — proceed immediately to the next processing stage.
</div>

**Readiness Indicators:**
1. Folding a leaf in half produces no audible snap from the central vein.
2. Squeezing a handful into a ball holds its shape without springing apart.

## 4. Stage 3. Freezing (Optional)

Ice crystals rupture cell walls from within, significantly accelerating juice extraction during subsequent rolling. Leaves can be stored in a freezer for up to `2 months`.

## 5. Stage 4. Cell Structure Disruption

The mechanical method determines the final grade and infusion density:

1. **Hand-rolling (small-leaf):** firmly roll `7–10` leaves between palms until darkened by released juice, forming rolls `~10 cm` long and `1–1.5 cm` thick.
2. **Kneading (large-leaf):** vigorously knead leaves in a deep bowl for `15–20 minutes`, breaking up clumps until juice coats the surface.
3. **Meat grinder (granulated / CTC-style):** pass withered leaves through a grinder fitted with a coarse plate.

## 6. Stage 5. Fermentation

Pack the processed mass into a vessel: place hand-rolled or kneaded leaves under a weight, or press granulated leaves down firmly by hand. Cover with a damp linen or cotton cloth (re-moistening as it dries) and keep in a warm place. In dry rooms, partially cover with a lid leaving an air gap.

* **Optimal temperature:** `22–26 °C`
* **Average duration:** `6–8 hours`
* **Completion signal:** emergence of a bright, rich fruity-floral aroma.

<div class="warning-box">
<strong>Thermal & Time Control:</strong> Below <code>15 °C</code>, fermentation slows down; above <code>30 °C</code>, soluble compounds turn insoluble and aroma degrades. Do not over-ferment — once the fruity aroma fades into a musty smell, the batch is spoiled.
</div>

## 7. Stage 6. Drying

Slice rolled leaf logs into `~0.5 cm` segments before drying (leaving them whole yields large-leaf tea with a lighter infusion). Spread in a `1 cm` layer on a parchment-lined baking tray.

1. **Oven drying:** start at `100 °C` for `1–1.5 hours` (traditional Chinese processing uses `125–150 °C` for the first `10–20 minutes` to caramelize surface sugars and seal juices inside). Lower to `50–60 °C` to finish drying, stirring regularly.
2. **Moisture equalization:** transfer cooled tea into thin cotton or linen bags and rest in a dry, ventilated area or covered outdoor space for over `7 days`.

**Readiness Indicators:** tea leaves are brittle, snap crisply when pressed, and produce a dry rustling sound when stirred.

## 8. Stage 7. Storage & Aging

Store finished tea in airtight glass or food-grade plastic containers in a dry, dark place. "Dry fermentation" continues during storage, rounding out the flavor profile. Allow **at least 1 month** of aging before the first brew.
