Sauerkraut

1. Ingredients

Quantities for one batch:

  • White cabbage, cleaned and trimmed — 2 kg;
  • Carrot — 200–300 g;
  • Table salt — 36–54 g;
  • Sugar — 1–2 tsp (optional).
Salt quantity note: One tablespoon, using the measure applied in this recipe, corresponds to 18 g of salt. The specified quantity represents 1.8–2.7% of the prepared cabbage weight. For the first preparation, it is recommended to start with the lower amount and adjust according to taste preferences.

2. Equipment

  • Mixing bowl or other suitable mixing container;
  • Knife and cutting board;
  • Grater for the carrot;
  • Glass fermentation jar (a 3-litre jar is used in the documented method);
  • Rolling pin or another suitable tool for pressing the cabbage;
  • Long sticks for piercing the cabbage mixture;
  • Cloth for temporarily covering the jar;
  • Small glass jars for subsequent storage.

3. Ingredient Preparation

3.1. Selecting the Cabbage

Fresh, juicy, white and naturally sweet cabbage is selected.

The following characteristics are considered when evaluating a cabbage head:

  • Overall appearance and visual quality;
  • Firmness and retained turgor;
  • Juiciness, assessed in part by the characteristic crunch when the head is squeezed;
  • Ease of chewing the raw cabbage;
  • Sweetness and the absence of excessively coarse fibres.

Prolonged storage at a retail counter may be accompanied by wilting and drying of the outer leaves, loss of firmness and a looser cabbage structure.

Preference is given to cabbage with a pleasant texture and taste. Selection is not restricted to a particular variety or season: suitable raw material can be found at different times of the year.

3.2. Cleaning the Cabbage

Outer, dried, damaged and coarse leaves are removed, together with the hard central core of the cabbage stalk.

Fresh, tender and juicy leaves are retained for preparation.

Where composting is available, unsuitable trimmings are sent for composting.

Alternatively, clean coarse leaves and the cabbage core can be stored in a freezer and later used to prepare vegetable stock. The trimmings are washed and frozen beforehand. They are simmered for approximately 1–2 hours. After cooking, the solid remains are separated from the stock.

3.3. Washing and Cutting

The prepared cabbage is thoroughly washed with drinking water.

The cabbage head is divided into manageable sections for shredding. In the documented method, sections approximately 10 cm long are used.

The cabbage is shredded as finely as possible.

Fine shredding helps distribute the ingredients more evenly and increases the surface area of the cut cabbage.

3.4. Preparing the Carrot

The carrot is washed, peeled and grated using a fine grater.

The amount of carrot is selected according to the desired flavour. An excessive amount makes the finished cabbage predominantly orange and changes its original taste.

A moderate quantity is preferred in practice. Less carrot than specified in the basic recipe may also be used.

4. Mixing the Ingredients

The cabbage is combined with the carrot and salt.

If the volume of prepared cabbage exceeds the capacity of the mixing container, the ingredients are divided into several batches, maintaining the same proportions.

Salt is added in portions, taking into account the size of the mixing container and the convenience of mixing.

For the first preparation, part of the specified salt quantity can be added initially. The mixture is thoroughly combined and left for 10–15 minutes. Its taste is then assessed, and the remaining salt is added if necessary.

At this stage, the cabbage should have a pronounced salty taste, substantially different from that of a fresh salad. The perceived saltiness changes during subsequent preparation as the cabbage releases its juice.

4.1. Massaging the Cabbage

After mixing, the cabbage is thoroughly massaged by hand.

This operation requires considerable physical effort. The mixture is combined and massaged until the following observable changes occur:

  • Juice is released;
  • The initial volume decreases;
  • The cabbage structure becomes softer;
  • The mixture retains its characteristic crunch when squeezed.

The quality of mixing and massaging affects the uniformity of the prepared mixture and the amount of juice released.

4.2. Sugar and Additional Ingredients

Adding sugar is optional.

In earlier practical preparations, sugar was used while the process was still being established. Its addition was accompanied by more active fermentation and produced a good flavour. Later preparations adopted a simpler recipe without sugar.

When sugar is used, the possibility of increased fermentation activity and liquid release should be taken into account.

Additional seasonings may include coriander, cumin, whole black peppercorns, bay leaves, cloves and other ingredients.

However, the basic version is recommended with cabbage, carrot and salt only. This preserves the natural flavour of the main ingredient and provides a baseline against which the effect of additional ingredients can be assessed.

5. Packing into the Fermentation Jar

The prepared mixture is transferred into a glass jar.

The cabbage is added in small portions and thoroughly pressed down using a rolling pin or another suitable tool.

The mixture is packed as firmly as possible without using additional weights.

Headspace and liquid coverage: Sufficient headspace must be left at the top of the jar. During fermentation, gas and liquid are released, which may cause the contents to rise — insufficient headspace may result in brine overflowing from the jar. After packing, ensure that the cabbage is covered by its released juice: keeping the entire cabbage mass submerged in liquid is an essential condition of the documented method.

6. Fermentation

The basic method uses a room temperature of approximately 20–24 °C.

The active stage generally lasts around 3–4 days. The actual duration depends on the composition of the raw ingredients and the surrounding conditions.

6.1. Observing the Process

During the first 24 hours, gas production may be limited.

By the second day, gas formation usually becomes noticeable. The intensity of the process subsequently increases and then gradually decreases.

By the third or fourth day, the following changes are generally observed:

  • Gas production decreases;
  • The liquid level stabilises;
  • The cabbage develops a pronounced sauerkraut flavour.
These signs are used to assess the progress of the process in practice. They do not, by themselves, confirm that fermentation is fully complete.

6.2. Piercing the Cabbage

During fermentation, the cabbage mixture is regularly pierced with long sticks down to the bottom of the jar.

In the documented method, this operation is performed approximately once every 3–4 hours during the active stage.

Piercing allows gas accumulated within the densely packed cabbage to escape.

Wooden or bamboo sticks can be used, such as ordinary chopsticks.

After piercing, the liquid level and condition of the cabbage should be checked.

6.3. Monitoring the Liquid Level

Throughout fermentation, it is important to keep the cabbage completely submerged in liquid.

If necessary, the brine level is adjusted. Drinking water was used for this purpose in earlier practical preparations.

However, adding plain water changes the salt concentration, so this should not be regarded as a universal method of adjusting the brine.

When filling the jar, sufficient headspace should be provided in advance to accommodate released juice and gas, minimising liquid loss.

6.4. End of the Active Stage

A decrease in gas production and stabilisation of the liquid level are used as indicators for moving to the next stage.

Taste is an additional criterion: the cabbage should develop a characteristic, pronounced sourness.

The documented method provides for subsequent storage in a tightly closed container in the refrigerator.

7. Transferring the Cabbage for Storage

After the active stage, the cabbage is transferred into smaller glass jars together with all the accumulated brine.

The jars are selected according to the intended consumption period after opening.

During transfer, the cabbage is pressed down again. The entire product must remain covered with brine.

If a small adjustment of the liquid level is required to cover the surface, the documented method has allowed the addition of a small amount of drinking water. The resulting change in brine concentration should be taken into account.

Before closing the jars, check that no pieces of cabbage protrude above the liquid.

Tightly fitting lids are used.

The primary storage method is refrigeration.

A small amount of mustard oil was also used on the surface of the product as an additional measure. This is optional and is not considered a substitute for keeping the cabbage fully submerged in brine or storing it under refrigeration.

8. Storage and Consumption

The finished product is stored in tightly closed glass containers in the refrigerator.

All the cabbage must remain submerged in brine.

For convenient consumption, smaller jars are preferred, sized for a single serving or several meals.

Practical observations indicate that the product can retain good flavour during extended refrigerated storage, reportedly for up to approximately six months. This period reflects practical experience and is not a guaranteed shelf life or confirmation of microbiological safety.

After opening, the contents are recommended to be consumed within 1–3 days.

9. Practical Notes

The following operations are particularly important for achieving a predictable result with this preparation method:

  • Selecting fresh, juicy and sweet cabbage.
  • Removing coarse leaves and the hard cabbage core.
  • Shredding the cabbage as finely as possible.
  • Adding salt gradually and mixing thoroughly.
  • Massaging the cabbage intensively until juice is released.
  • Pressing the mixture firmly into the glass jar.
  • Providing sufficient headspace at the top of the jar.
  • Regularly piercing the mixture during active fermentation.
  • Ensuring that the cabbage remains completely submerged in brine.
  • Refrigerating the product in tightly closed containers.

For the first preparation, the basic recipe without sugar or additional seasonings is recommended. Once a result has been obtained, the quantity of carrot, sugar and seasonings can be adjusted gradually, changing one element at a time.

10. Editorial Information and Limitations

Storage Conditions
